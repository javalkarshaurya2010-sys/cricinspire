Nine fielders, one bowler, and one wicketkeeper make up the fielding side. There is no fixed layout. The captain moves fielders around based on the batter and the situation.

The field is split into two sides: the **off side** (the side the batter's bat faces when they stand) and the **leg side** (the other side).

Some positions you will hear a lot:

- **Wicketkeeper.** Stands behind the stumps. Like a catcher, but with gloves and no mask.
- **Slip.** Stands next to the keeper to catch edges off the bat.
- **Cover and mid-off.** Guard the off side in front of the batter.
- **Mid-on and midwicket.** Guard the leg side in front of the batter.
- **Square leg.** Stands level with the batter on the leg side.
- **Fine leg.** Far behind the batter on the leg side, stopping fine glances.

In T20 cricket there are rules that limit how many fielders can stand far from the bat in the first six overs. This is called the powerplay and it pushes batters to hit big.
